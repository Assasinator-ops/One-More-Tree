What we actually do

A tree is the easy part. The forest is the work.

Anyone can put a sapling in the ground for a photograph. Getting that sapling through its first dry summer — and its tenth — is what separates a planting day from a forest. That is the only part we are interested in.

We work with a single measure of success: the share of trees still alive, healthy and self-seeding five years after planting. Everything else — the nurseries, the drones, the stewardship contracts — exists to move that number.

Six things we do, in order

A restoration programme is a sequence, not a single event. Each stage below depends on the one before it.

01

Native woodland restoration

Drought-adapted oak, pine and juniper planted where soil, aspect and rainfall data say they will actually hold.

02

Urban pocket forests

Dense Miyawaki-style planting in city neighbourhoods, where mature canopies can cool a street by up to 8°C.

03

Community stewardship

Five-year guardianship agreements with the people who live beside the plot — and a paid local steward for each one.

04

Drone & satellite monitoring

Multispectral NDVI mapping that flags a stressed tree weeks before the eye can see it, so watering goes where it is needed.

05

Seed provenance & nurseries

A regional seed bank matched to watershed, altitude and soil type. Local genetics are the foundation of a forest that lasts.

06

Carbon & biodiversity research

Published measurement of what Mediterranean dryland forest really stores — most of it below ground, in root systems that survive fire.

Three projects, three different problems

Mountains, city streets and riverbanks each fail in their own way. So does the planting that repairs them.

Andalusia · 2024—ongoing

Sierra Nevada highlands

10,000 stone pines and native juniper across three altitude bands, on slopes that had been bare since a 2005 fire. Micro-catchments around every seedling harvest morning condensation.

10,000 trees · 42 ha · 96% survival

Madrid · 2023—ongoing

Madrid pocket forests

Nine neighbourhood micro-forests on unused hardstanding, planted at high density. In two years they are self-sustaining — and measurably cooler at street level.

9 sites · 3,100 trees · −3.4°C street level

Extremadura · 2022—ongoing

Guadiana river corridor

Riparian ash, willow and poplar reconnecting a floodplain cut into fragments by irrigation channels. This is the corridor that lets wildlife move again.

26,000 trees · 78 ha · 11 km reconnected
