Thirty-eight active restoration sites across Spain and Portugal. Three of them define the range of what we do — mountain, city and river — and every one of them is monitored, funded and reported on individually.

Flagship project

Sierra Nevada: 10,000 trees on burnt ground

A 2005 brushfire left 400 hectares of high-altitude slope bare. Without root systems to hold it, the topsoil was moving downhill with every storm. In spring 2024 we planted 10,000 stone pines and native juniper across three altitude bands.

Fifty-two volunteers, twelve local forest guardians, six nursery botanists and three coordinators worked seven days. Where the slope exceeded 25°, we cut micro-catchments around every seedling to trap morning condensation and run-off — on that terrain it is the difference between a tree and a stick.

Two seasons on, survival is 96%. The monitoring station at the 1,800 m band now logs soil moisture and canopy temperature hourly, and local school groups have planted their own micro-groves inside the site.

Before you fund or volunteer

A restoration plot is a commitment measured in years, not a day of digging. A plot is considered live from the day the first sapling goes in — and the way we count that day matters: a tree is only counted once it has survived one full summer, so a planting week in October will not show up in our numbers until the following August.

That is why the numbers on this site lag the planting season, and why we can tell you exactly how many trees died between one survey and the next — we do not count a tree until it has earned the right to stay.

  • When we count a tree — one full summer after planting, not the day it goes in the ground.
  • What the numbers include — only trees still alive, healthy and self-seeding, on the survey date.
  • What they leave out — saplings that died before their first summer, and planting days that have not yet reached their first survey.
  • Why that matters — so a figure is a promise kept, not a promise made.

Not all of it is pretty — but all of it is true.

How a plot is monitored

Survival rates are only meaningful if someone independent checks them. Ours are sampled the same way every season, by the same protocol, in every plot.

  • Drone survey — multispectral NDVI mapping over every plot each spring and autumn, at 3 cm resolution.
  • Ground transects — randomised 10×10 m sample plots, counted by hand by an independent ecological surveyor.
  • Soil carbon cores — taken at year 0, 3 and 5 to measure below-ground sequestration, not just trunk growth.
  • Watering response — NDVI stress alerts dispatch stewards to specific trees within 72 hours.
  • Publication — raw plot data, including losses, released every January.
