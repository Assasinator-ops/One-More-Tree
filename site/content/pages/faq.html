Planting and survival

Because "trees planted" is measured on the day everyone is happiest, before a single dry summer has tested the work. It is the easiest number to inflate and the least useful one for deciding whether a forest will exist in thirty years. Survival after three summers is harder to produce, sometimes unflattering, and it is the only figure that tells you whether the money worked.

From the land, not from a catalogue. Six weeks of survey before anything is ordered: soil depth and pH, slope aspect, rainfall history, fire record and the existing local seed bank. We then plant a mix of four to fourteen species from the same watershed and altitude band. Local genetics matter more than most people expect — a tree from foreign seed can survive, but the insects, fungi and birds that evolved alongside it will not follow.

They are counted as losses and reported as such. A plot with 91% survival has 9% failure in the published number. We do not replant automatically: if a species consistently fails at a given altitude, that is information about the site, and planting it again wastes money. Twice a year we also publish any corrective replanting, and why it was needed.

Fire is part of Mediterranean ecology, and we plant for it. The species we use — cork oak, stone pine, native juniper — resprout or seed after fire precisely because they evolved with it. We also create firebreaks between plots and thin the understory on a schedule. The far greater risk is a hot, dry decade, which is exactly why we invest in root depth and micro-catchments.

We accept corporate funding, but we do not sell carbon offsets ahead of the measurement. A company can fund a plot and receive the quarterly monitoring reports, including the trees that died. What we will not do is pre-sell a verified credit for carbon that has not yet been measured in soil cores. That distinction is the reason some procurement teams walk away, and we are comfortable with that.

Money and transparency

80%. Of every euro, 44% buys planting, nurseries and seed collection, and 26% funds the five-year stewardship stipends that keep the trees alive. The remaining 20% covers monitoring, soil analysis, programme staff and administration — the parts that make the results independently checkable. Full audited accounts are published every year.

€18 funds the planting of roughly one hectare's worth of seedlings at our average density. €90 funds one hectare's stewardship for a year, including the watering that carries it through August. €450 funds one hectare for its full five-year establishment period. These are averages across sites; steep terrain costs more and urban plots cost considerably more.

Volunteering and working with us

None. Volunteers work in pairs with a trained guardian, and the first morning covers the technique, the tools and the site. What actually matters is stamina and patience — a planting day is six hours of bent-over work on uneven ground, and the correct depth and spacing matters more than speed.

Yes, and it is usually the most useful thing a company can do — a full plot funded for five years beats a hundred volunteers for one afternoon. Sponsors receive quarterly monitoring reports and a named site. Teams are also welcome to plant on their own plot, but we would rather you funded the stewardship than the photographs.
