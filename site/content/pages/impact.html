Survival is the only metric that matters

Most reforestation reporting counts trees planted. That number is easy to produce and tells you almost nothing: it is measured on the day everyone is happiest, before a single dry summer has tested the work. We publish survival instead, because it is the number that determines whether a forest exists in thirty years.

Our data is not perfect, and we say so. Some plots are on terrain that cannot be fully surveyed by drone. Some species are counted in seedling clusters rather than individuals. Where a figure carries an uncertainty, the report notes it.

Why below-ground carbon is the story

Mediterranean dryland forest stores an unusually large share of its carbon beneath the soil, in extensive root systems, rather than in trunks and canopy. Those roots also survive brushfire and regenerate after drought. The carbon they hold stays locked for centuries, which is precisely what makes it worth financing.

We take soil cores at year 0, 3 and 5 rather than extrapolating from trunk diameter. That is slower and more expensive. It is also the only version of the claim we are prepared to publish.

Biodiversity, not just biomass

A forest of identical trees is a crop. We plant between four and fourteen species per plot, chosen for the insects, fungi and birds that depend on them — and we survey those too: 214 invertebrate species and 61 bird species recorded across our sites in 2025.

Two of our plots are now self-seeding, meaning the forest is regenerating without us. That is the point at which restoration becomes restoration.

Where the money goes

Of every euro donated, 80% reaches the field. The rest funds the monitoring, soil analysis and administration that make the results trustworthy. Our accounts are audited annually and published in full.

  • 44% — planting, nurseries and seed collection
  • 26% — five-year stewardship stipends and watering
  • 10% — drone, field and independent survey monitoring
  • 12% — programme management and field staff
  • 8% — fundraising and administration
